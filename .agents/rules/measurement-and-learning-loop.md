# Measurement & Learning Loop Governance

## Purpose

Measurement is not a reporting layer only. It is the feedback mechanism that connects execution results back to Strategic Intelligence.

## Decision Boundary

For broad, ambiguous, cross-functional, or decision-oriented work, preserve this order:

`Business Objective → Strategic Decision → Diagnosis → Evidence/Hypothesis → Measurement Design → Execution → Observed Result → Interpretation → Learning → Strategic Intelligence`

Measurement specialists may define instrumentation, metric calculations, attribution analysis, dashboards, experiment analysis, and reporting. They must not silently replace the strategic objective or declare a channel, campaign, creative, or growth tactic to be the strategic answer.

## Metric Discipline

- A KPI must have a clear relationship to the business objective or decision it is intended to inform.
- Reach, impressions, views, clicks, engagement, leads, and similar activity metrics are not automatically business outcomes.
- A metric moving in the desired direction does not by itself prove that the intervention caused the change.
- Attribution is a measurement model with assumptions and limitations, not unquestionable ground truth.
- Benchmarks, thresholds, ratios, sample sizes, test durations, and targets are context-dependent unless supported by relevant evidence.
- Never invent performance data, conversion rates, CAC, LTV, ROAS, revenue, margins, lift, or customer outcomes.

## Evidence Classification

Every material measurement conclusion should distinguish:

- **FACT** — directly observed or reliably sourced measurement.
- **OBSERVATION** — pattern visible in the data without causal interpretation.
- **INFERENCE** — reasoned interpretation of observations.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — explanation that still requires testing or evidence.
- **BENCHMARK** — external or historical comparison, clearly identified as such.
- **RECOMMENDATION** — decision proposed from the evidence.

Do not present an inference or hypothesis as a measured fact.

## Diagnostic Discipline

When performance changes, investigate the full causal system before prescribing optimization. Potential contributors can include:

- audience or customer fit
- offer, pricing, or packaging
- positioning and message
- creative or content
- channel/media fit
- landing/conversion experience
- sales follow-up and qualification
- product/service delivery
- tracking and data quality
- seasonality, market conditions, or external factors

A result such as lower CPA or higher CTR is not sufficient evidence that the latest creative or targeting change caused the improvement.

## Experimentation

Use controlled learning where practical:

`Baseline → Hypothesis → One meaningful change → Test → Compare → Interpret → Decision → Learning`

Prefer finite stopping conditions. Do not create an endless optimization loop merely because a metric can still be improved.

Possible decisions:

- **KEEP** — evidence supports continuation.
- **CHANGE** — evidence supports a specific modification.
- **TEST** — uncertainty is material and experimentation is appropriate.
- **INVESTIGATE** — data quality or causal ambiguity prevents a reliable decision.
- **STOP** — evidence indicates the intervention is not justified.
- **ESCALATE** — findings challenge the strategic diagnosis or objective.

## Strategic Feedback Loop

Results must be returned to Strategic Intelligence when they:

- contradict a strategic assumption;
- reveal a different bottleneck or root cause;
- materially change audience, offer, positioning, channel role, or business priority;
- expose a previously unknown constraint or opportunity;
- provide evidence strong enough to change the recommended workstream.

Specialists must preserve the original strategic context when reporting results and explicitly identify what changed.

## Attribution & Causality Guardrails

Do not equate:

- correlation with causation;
- last-click credit with total business impact;
- competitor performance with competitor effectiveness;
- platform-reported conversion with independently verified business outcome;
- short-term lift with durable growth;
- statistical movement with strategic significance.

Where causal confidence is limited, say so and recommend the smallest useful next investigation or experiment.

## Quality Gate

Before finalizing a measurement or growth recommendation, verify:

1. The metric traces back to a business objective or decision.
2. Observed results are separated from interpretation and hypothesis.
3. Data quality and attribution limitations are acknowledged where material.
4. No unsupported business or performance numbers are presented as fact.
5. Root causes are not inferred from a single metric movement without sufficient evidence.
6. The recommendation is proportionate to evidence and uncertainty.
7. Findings that change the strategic diagnosis are escalated to Strategic Intelligence.
8. The loop ends in a decision or learning, not merely a dashboard/report.
