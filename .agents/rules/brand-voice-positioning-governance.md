# Brand Voice & Positioning Governance

This rule governs `messaging-positioning`, `messaging-positioning-workshop`, and brand-voice guidance.

## Strategic Boundary

Positioning is a strategic choice; messaging and voice are downstream communication systems.

For broad, ambiguous, cross-functional, or decision-oriented requests:

`Strategic Intelligence → Evidence/Research → Positioning → Messaging/Voice → Execution`

Do not let a messaging template, workshop exercise, or brand-voice preference silently determine the business strategy.

## Evidence Before Positioning

- Competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value, target market, and category must be grounded in available evidence or explicitly marked as assumptions/hypotheses.
- Customer language may inform positioning, but customer statements are not automatically market truth.
- Competitor claims are evidence of what competitors communicate, not proof that those claims are true or effective.
- A differentiation claim must be supportable by the actual product/service and available proof.
- If critical evidence is missing, state the gap instead of filling it with plausible-sounding assumptions.

## Framework Discipline

April Dunford, Geoffrey Moore, JTBD, Message House, and other frameworks are decision aids, not mandatory formulas.

- Select the framework that fits the diagnosed problem.
- Do not require a fixed number of attributes, pillars, proof points, personas, interviews, workshop minutes, or validation participants unless the specific context/evidence supports that choice.
- Quantification is preferred when genuinely measurable, but never invent a metric merely to make positioning sound stronger.
- Example numbers and example positioning statements must remain clearly illustrative.

## Voice vs Positioning

Keep these concepts separate:

- **Positioning:** why this offering is valuable, for whom, relative to alternatives.
- **Messaging:** what aspects of that positioning are communicated and how.
- **Brand voice:** the recognizable manner/style in which the brand communicates.
- **Tone:** contextual variation of voice by situation, audience, and journey stage.

A warmer, sharper, more premium, more playful, or more formal voice does not compensate for weak positioning, weak proof, poor offer-market fit, or an incorrect audience.

## Persona Messaging

Persona-specific messaging may change emphasis, language, examples, and proof, but should not silently create a different core positioning.

If different audiences genuinely require materially different value propositions, flag this as a strategic segmentation/positioning question for `strategic-intelligence`.

## Validation

Positioning validation should distinguish:

- stakeholder opinion
- customer language
- observed buyer behavior
- experiment result
- business outcome

A positive reaction to wording is not proof of market demand or commercial effectiveness.

## Handoff Contract

When returning positioning/messaging work downstream, preserve:

1. Strategic objective.
2. Target audience and journey stage.
3. Diagnosed problem/opportunity.
4. Positioning decision and alternatives considered.
5. Evidence and confidence.
6. Required proof and unresolved gaps.
7. Channel/context constraints.

If new evidence materially changes any of these, return the issue to `strategic-intelligence` rather than silently changing the strategic diagnosis.

## Quality Gate

Before finalizing:
- Separate positioning, messaging, voice, and tone.
- Separate facts from hypotheses and creative choices.
- No unsupported superiority/outcome claims.
- No fabricated customer language, metrics, testimonials, or competitor facts.
- Framework defaults are not presented as universal laws.
- Strategic conflicts are escalated upstream.
