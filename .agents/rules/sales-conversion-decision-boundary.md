# Sales & Conversion Decision Boundary

## Purpose

Sales and conversion evidence must be treated as part of the business system, not as an isolated persuasion layer. This rule connects acquisition, qualification, sales execution, closing, and customer outcomes back to Strategic Intelligence.

## Operating Order

For broad, ambiguous, cross-functional, or decision-oriented work:

`Business Objective → Strategic Diagnosis → Customer/Journey Evidence → Funnel Diagnosis → Sales/Conversion Evidence → Specialist Analysis → Decision → Measurement → Learning`

Do not assume the bottleneck is advertising, lead volume, sales technique, objection handling, or closing.

## Funnel Diagnostic Discipline

Treat funnel stages as diagnostic locations, not automatic goals.

A conversion metric is meaningful only when its event, denominator, segment, time window, and source are clear. Do not compare conversion rates when the underlying definitions differ.

When a stage underperforms, investigate adjacent and upstream/downstream causes, including:

- audience or lead quality;
- demand and intent;
- offer, pricing, or packaging;
- positioning and message;
- channel/source fit;
- landing or inquiry experience;
- qualification criteria;
- response speed and follow-up process;
- discovery and needs diagnosis;
- proof, trust, and risk perception;
- proposal/commercial process;
- product/service readiness and delivery;
- measurement or CRM data quality.

A lower lead-to-sale rate does not by itself prove that salespeople need better scripts. A high lead volume does not prove healthy demand.

## Sales Evidence Hierarchy

Prefer evidence in this order when available:

1. First-party CRM and transaction records
2. Sales call/demo transcripts and deal histories
3. Buyer/customer feedback
4. Proposal, pricing, and stage data
5. Marketing/channel attribution data
6. Seller opinion or anecdotal recollection

Separate:

- **FACT** — verified transaction or CRM evidence;
- **OBSERVATION** — directly visible pattern;
- **BUYER-STATED REASON** — explicit reason given by buyer/customer;
- **INFERENCE** — interpretation of evidence;
- **HYPOTHESIS** — explanation requiring validation;
- **BENCHMARK** — external comparison;
- **RECOMMENDATION** — proposed action.

Frequency is evidence of recurrence, not proof of importance or causality. Consider segment, deal value, stage, fit, recency, and evidence quality.

## Qualification & Lead Quality

Do not define a "good lead" from channel source alone.

Qualification should reflect the actual buying decision and business model. Possible dimensions include need/problem fit, use case, authority or buying role where relevant, timing, commercial fit, readiness, and ability to progress.

Do not invent an ICP, qualification threshold, lead score, or MQL/SQL definition. If the business has no validated definition, mark it as an open decision or hypothesis.

## Follow-Up & Sales Process

When diagnosing sales performance, inspect the process rather than assuming motivation or skill is the cause.

Where evidence exists, examine:

- handoff quality from marketing to sales;
- response and follow-up behavior;
- qualification consistency;
- discovery quality;
- stage progression and abandonment;
- proposal/commercial friction;
- objection patterns;
- decision-maker involvement;
- no-decision patterns;
- reasons for stalled or lost opportunities.

Do not prescribe a fixed follow-up cadence, number of touches, meeting count, or sales-cycle duration as a universal rule. Adapt to the buying cycle and evidence.

## Objections Are Diagnostic Signals

`objection-library` and `win-loss-reasons` should be used to identify what buyers actually say and what deal evidence shows.

An objection can signal:

- genuine product limitation;
- offer/pricing mismatch;
- positioning or message mismatch;
- insufficient proof/trust;
- poor qualification;
- sales-handling issue;
- procurement or operational constraint;
- market readiness problem.

Do not treat every objection as something that should be "overcome." Sometimes the correct strategic response is to change the offer, qualify differently, improve proof, or stop pursuing a segment.

## Conversion Economics

Metrics such as CAC, CPL, CPA, ROAS, LTV, payback period, revenue per lead, win rate, and margin require actual business inputs and clear definitions.

Never fabricate these values or impose universal thresholds. When inputs are missing, identify the missing variables and show the calculation logic without pretending the result is known.

## Sales Enablement Boundary

`sales-deck` may structure the buyer narrative, `objection-library` may analyze objections and handling, and `win-loss-reasons` may synthesize deal outcomes.

These specialists may:

- analyze supplied sales evidence;
- identify patterns and friction;
- produce enablement material;
- suggest hypotheses and tests;
- surface upstream strategic implications.

They may not independently decide:

- the strategic objective;
- the target market or ICP without evidence;
- positioning or value proposition;
- pricing policy;
- primary acquisition channel;
- sales motion priority;
- that a sales intervention is the highest-leverage solution.

Those decisions remain with Strategic Intelligence and the appropriate upstream specialists.

## Measurement & Experimentation

For sales or conversion experiments, define before execution where practical:

- hypothesis;
- intervention;
- decision-stage or funnel event;
- primary outcome metric;
- relevant guardrails;
- evidence window or stopping condition;
- decision rule.

Do not optimize only for intermediate activity such as calls booked, meetings held, or leads contacted when the business objective depends on later outcomes.

Interpret results cautiously. A change in win rate, conversion, or sales-cycle length can be affected by mix, seasonality, deal composition, market conditions, or measurement changes.

## Strategic Feedback Loop

Return findings to Strategic Intelligence when sales/conversion evidence:

- contradicts the assumed audience or ICP;
- reveals a different root cause;
- changes perceived value or purchase criteria;
- challenges positioning, offer, pricing, or channel assumptions;
- reveals a material qualification or sales-motion constraint;
- changes the expected economics of acquisition;
- creates a new strategic opportunity.

Do not continue optimizing the local sales tactic while ignoring evidence that the strategic diagnosis has changed.

## Quality Gate

Before finalizing a sales/conversion recommendation:

1. The business objective and relevant decision are clear.
2. The conversion event and denominator are defined.
3. Buyer-stated reasons are separated from inferred causes.
4. CRM/transaction evidence is distinguished from seller opinion.
5. No unsupported rates, thresholds, economics, or customer claims are presented as facts.
6. Upstream and downstream causes were considered before prescribing a local fix.
7. Objections are treated as diagnostic evidence, not automatically as persuasion problems.
8. Specialist findings that challenge strategy are escalated to Strategic Intelligence.
9. The recommendation ends in a decision, test, investigation, or learning—not merely more sales activity.
