# Content Planning Decision Boundary

## Purpose

Apply this rule to editorial calendars, content roadmaps, blog planning, newsletter planning, social planning, and other content-planning work.

## Strategic boundary

Content planning is an execution/planning layer. For broad, ambiguous, cross-functional, or decision-oriented requests, consume `strategic-intelligence` first.

Strategic Intelligence should establish, when relevant:
- business objective and decision
- diagnosed bottleneck or opportunity
- target audience and journey
- positioning and message priorities
- evidence and research gaps
- channel/media role
- constraints, capacity, and dependencies
- business outcome and measurement

Do not treat "we need more content" as proof that more content is the correct intervention.

## Planning principle

Use this order:

**Business objective → diagnosis → evidence → content role → content choices → calendar → production → measurement → learning**

A calendar must explain why planned content exists. A topic, format, platform, frequency, or publishing cadence is not a strategy by itself.

## Framework and ratio guardrails

Frameworks such as Perceptions, content pillars, funnel mapping, repurposing, 70/30 content mixes, reactive buffers, pillar coverage rules, or fixed publishing cadences are methods/templates, not universal laws.

- Use them when they fit the diagnosis.
- Treat numeric ratios, frequencies, counts, timing, and cadence as benchmarks, starting hypotheses, or user constraints unless supported by evidence.
- Do not force a target ratio merely to make the calendar look balanced.
- If the best plan is fewer pieces, fewer pillars, or no calendar at all, say so.

## Channel and distribution guardrails

Do not assume a channel is appropriate because it is popular, already used, or present in a template.

Select distribution based on evidence about audience behavior, intent, demand, funnel role, competitive context, organic/paid opportunity, conversion path, measurement, content requirements, operational complexity, and team capacity.

## Evidence guardrails

Do not fabricate:
- audience preferences
- performance results
- customer stories or quotes
- competitor activity
- market trends
- search demand
- engagement or conversion metrics
- content-attributed revenue

Separate FACT, OBSERVATION, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS, BENCHMARK, and RECOMMENDATION.

## Measurement

Do not optimize the calendar for publishing volume, impressions, reach, or engagement alone. Connect content measurement to the role the content is intended to play in the customer journey and business objective.

Where attribution is weak, state the limitation rather than claiming causal business impact.

## Handoff

When handing planned content to `blog`, `social-posts`, `email`, `copywriting`, or other execution skills, preserve the strategic objective, audience, diagnosis, message priority, channel role, evidence status, constraints, and measurement intent.

Execution specialists may improve the asset. They must not silently change the strategic decision. If new evidence materially changes the diagnosis, return to `strategic-intelligence`.
