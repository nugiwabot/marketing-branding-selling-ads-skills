# Pricing & Monetization Governance

## Purpose

Govern pricing, packaging, value metrics, discounts, and monetization decisions so they are treated as commercial strategy rather than generic pricing templates.

## Pricing Is Multi-Constraint

A pricing decision should consider, where material:

- customer value and willingness-to-pay evidence;
- demand elasticity and volume effects;
- cost-to-serve and economic floor;
- gross/contribution margin requirements;
- competitor and substitute reference prices;
- perceived differentiation and positioning;
- customer segment differences;
- acquisition channel and sales motion;
- retention/expansion effects;
- packaging and feature/capacity constraints;
- implementation or switching costs;
- legal/regulatory constraints where applicable;
- billing, discounting, procurement, and commercial complexity.

Do not treat one pricing philosophy as universally correct.

## Cost-Plus, Competitive, and Value-Based Pricing

Value-based pricing can be a useful primary approach when credible customer-value and willingness-to-pay evidence exists.

Cost information remains strategically relevant for minimum viable economics, contribution requirements, capacity decisions, and downside protection.

Competitor pricing is a reference point, not proof of customer value or an instruction to match.

When evidence is weak, use a hybrid analysis rather than declaring one method universally superior.

## Value Metric

A value metric should be tested for:

- customer understanding;
- relationship to value received;
- growth with customer success where appropriate;
- predictability and budgetability;
- resistance to gaming;
- impact on adoption and expansion;
- alignment with cost-to-serve.

Do not assume seat-based, usage-based, outcome-based, flat-fee, or hybrid pricing is optimal without evidence.

## Packaging

Three tiers can be useful, but there is no universal optimal number of tiers.

Determine packaging from:

- meaningful customer segments or use cases;
- willingness-to-pay differences;
- product maturity/capability;
- value metric;
- sales complexity;
- upgrade/expansion logic;
- operational/service differences.

Do not require a middle tier to become the majority choice or use fixed tier price multipliers as laws. Those may be test assumptions.

## Segmentation

Segmented pricing may be appropriate when customers receive materially different value, have different willingness to pay, or incur different service costs.

Evaluate fairness, legal constraints, operational complexity, reference-price effects, and customer trust before implementing segment-specific pricing.

Do not infer willingness to pay from demographic labels alone.

## Pricing Research

When pricing is uncertain, use multiple evidence sources where practical:

1. observed purchase/transaction behavior;
2. renewal, expansion, downgrade, and churn behavior;
3. sales negotiation and discount data;
4. customer interviews and stated willingness to pay;
5. controlled price/package tests where feasible;
6. competitor/substitute reference pricing.

Recognize that stated willingness to pay may differ from revealed willingness to pay.

## Price Changes

A proposed price increase or decrease should consider:

- affected segments;
- price elasticity uncertainty;
- churn/retention risk;
- acquisition effects;
- perceived value;
- competitor response;
- contractual constraints;
- margin and volume sensitivity;
- communication and migration strategy.

Model scenarios when the decision is material. Do not assert that a particular percentage increase is optimal without evidence.

## Discounting

Discounts are not automatically harmless conversion tools.

Analyze:

- reason for the discount;
- deal economics;
- frequency;
- segment eligibility;
- reference-price effects;
- precedent and expectation-setting;
- renewal impact;
- sales-cycle impact.

Measure whether discounting increases incremental value or merely transfers margin.

## Monetization Decision Test

For significant pricing/packaging decisions, answer:

1. What customer value is being monetized?
2. What evidence supports willingness to pay?
3. What are the economics and constraints?
4. What strategic position does the price reinforce?
5. What behavior will the pricing model encourage or discourage?
6. What segments/use cases gain or lose?
7. What alternatives exist?
8. What would disconfirm the recommendation?
9. What is the lowest-cost validation test?
10. What measurement determines whether the change worked?

## Specialist Boundary

`pricing-packaging` may analyze pricing models, packages, value metrics, and monetization options.

It must not independently decide the overall business objective, target market, product strategy, or channel strategy. Escalate when pricing evidence materially changes positioning, segment choice, offer architecture, customer economics, or strategic priorities.

## Quality Gate

Before finalizing:

- [ ] Pricing method is justified by context, not doctrine.
- [ ] Cost, value, competition, and customer evidence are considered where relevant.
- [ ] Packaging count and tier ratios are treated as hypotheses unless validated.
- [ ] Pricing claims and benchmarks are sourced or labeled.
- [ ] Economics are explicit enough for the decision.
- [ ] Segmentation and fairness/legal implications are considered when relevant.
- [ ] Price-change risks and scenarios are visible.
- [ ] Discounting effects are considered where relevant.
- [ ] Alternatives and validation tests are stated.
