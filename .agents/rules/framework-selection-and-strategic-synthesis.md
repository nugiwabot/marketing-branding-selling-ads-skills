# Framework Selection & Strategic Synthesis

## Purpose

Govern framework selection inside Strategic Intelligence. Frameworks are diagnostic instruments chosen for a decision, not a checklist of theories to mention.

## Selection Principle

Use the **minimum sufficient framework set** that materially improves the decision.

Select a framework only when:

- the decision question is clear;
- its assumptions match the business context;
- required data is available or can be researched;
- the framework adds explanatory or decision value;
- its limitations are understood.

Never add frameworks merely to make a strategy look comprehensive.

## Decision → Framework Guidance

Use this as a routing guide, not a rigid mapping.

| Decision / Question | Potentially useful frameworks | Typical output |
|---|---|---|
| How does the business create/capture value? | Business Model Canvas, operating model analysis, value chain | Business model logic, gaps, dependencies |
| What customer problem/value matters? | JTBD, Value Proposition Canvas, customer journey | Jobs, pains, gains, value proposition implications |
| Who should we prioritize? | STP, ICP/segment analysis, JTBD segmentation | Segment attractiveness, fit, priority |
| How is the market structured? | Five Forces, competitor/substitute analysis | Structural pressure, bargaining power, substitutes |
| What external forces matter? | PESTEL, scenario analysis | External risks/opportunities |
| Where can we grow? | Ansoff, market expansion analysis, growth-loop analysis | Growth vectors and trade-offs |
| How should we compete? | Positioning frameworks, strategic group mapping, competitor alternatives | Competitive choice and differentiation |
| What brand meaning should we build? | Brand identity/equity frameworks, positioning, message architecture | Brand direction, associations, proof |
| How should we price/package? | Value metric analysis, willingness-to-pay evidence, pricing architecture, unit economics | Pricing/packaging hypotheses and economics |
| Where is the funnel constrained? | Customer journey, funnel analysis, cohort analysis, AAARRR where appropriate | Bottleneck and stage-specific interventions |
| Which channels/media should we use? | Channel-market fit analysis, media landscape research, funnel economics | Primary/supporting/experimental channel roles |
| Which initiatives should we prioritize? | Decision matrix, impact/feasibility analysis, scenario/sensitivity analysis | Prioritized choices and trade-offs |
| Which offerings/segments should receive resources? | Portfolio analysis where data supports it, contribution economics, strategic fit | Resource allocation options |

## Framework Stacking

Frameworks may be combined when each answers a distinct part of the same decision.

Example:

`PESTEL → Five Forces → customer/JTBD → positioning → channel economics → prioritized choice`

But avoid redundant frameworks that produce the same insight.

The output should synthesize across frameworks rather than present disconnected framework sections.

## Framework Limitations

Before applying a framework, consider whether its assumptions fit.

Examples:

- BCG-style portfolio logic needs meaningful relative market position and growth context; do not fabricate these values.
- Quantitative matrices are not superior to qualitative reasoning when the underlying data is weak.
- SWOT is a synthesis aid, not root-cause analysis and not a substitute for evidence.
- A 2x2 positioning map reflects selected dimensions; it is not objective market truth.
- Personas are representations, not evidence of actual customer behavior.
- Funnel models can hide non-linear journeys; use the model appropriate to the buying process.
- TAM/SAM/SOM ranges are estimates unless grounded in credible evidence.
- Benchmark tables can support comparison but cannot substitute for business-specific economics.

## Cross-Framework Coherence Test

After applying multiple frameworks, test whether the conclusions agree across:

1. Customer desirability
2. Market attractiveness/structure
3. Competitive differentiation
4. Business capability/operational feasibility
5. Unit economics/commercial viability
6. Brand credibility
7. Channel/distribution feasibility
8. Measurement/learning ability

When frameworks conflict, do not force convergence. State the tension and identify the decision that resolves it.

## Strategic Synthesis Output

The synthesis should answer:

> Given the evidence, constraints, alternatives, and trade-offs, what should the business do, why, and what must be true for it to work?

A high-quality synthesis contains:

- decision;
- evidence basis;
- strategic logic;
- trade-offs;
- assumptions;
- dependencies;
- risks;
- validation plan;
- measurement/decision gate.

Avoid outputs that are simply a sequence of framework summaries.

## Anti-Pattern

Do not write:

`BMC says X → SWOT says Y → 5 Forces says Z → therefore run Instagram.`

Instead derive the decision from converging evidence and explicit trade-offs.
