# Marketing Legal, Compliance & Reputational Risk

## Purpose

Provide a mandatory risk screen for strategic marketing, branding, selling, advertising, and promotional decisions when legal, regulatory, privacy, consumer-protection, contractual, platform, or reputational exposure may be material.

This is a risk-detection and escalation rule, not a substitute for qualified legal counsel.

## When to Apply

Apply the screen when work involves, for example:

- regulated products or services;
- financial/investment or return claims;
- health, medical, safety, or regulated professional claims;
- comparative or superiority advertising;
- pricing/discount promotions or material terms;
- testimonials, endorsements, influencer content, or customer data;
- privacy, tracking, consent, cookies, CRM, or lead capture;
- intellectual property, trademarks, images, or third-party content;
- contracts, exclusivity, licensing, or distributor/channel restrictions;
- platform advertising/publishing policies;
- sector-specific or jurisdiction-specific requirements;
- claims that could create material consumer, partner, or reputational risk.

## Claim Discipline

Before using a material claim, determine:

1. What exactly is being claimed?
2. What evidence supports it?
3. Who could reasonably interpret it as a factual promise?
4. What qualifiers or assumptions materially affect interpretation?
5. What jurisdiction or sector rules may apply?
6. Does the claim compare, guarantee, imply certainty, or create a material financial/health/safety expectation?

Never convert an estimate, benchmark, aspiration, testimonial, or model output into a factual guarantee.

## Financial / Property / Investment Claims

For property, investment, financial, yield, ROI, passive-income, appreciation, or similar claims:

- do not guarantee returns or capital appreciation;
- separate factual property characteristics from financial projections;
- show assumptions behind any calculation;
- distinguish gross yield, net yield, revenue, profit, cash flow, and return concepts;
- identify vacancies, operating costs, financing, taxes, fees, timing, and other material variables where relevant;
- use verified figures only;
- escalate jurisdiction-specific legal or advertising requirements when material.

A persuasive property message must not make an uncertain financial outcome sound certain.

## Privacy & Data

When customer/lead data or tracking is involved, identify:

- what data is collected;
- why it is collected;
- consent/lawful-basis requirements where applicable;
- retention/access controls;
- third-party processor/platform implications;
- whether the proposed marketing use is consistent with the stated purpose and applicable requirements.

Do not expose, infer, or fabricate private customer information.

## Comparative Advertising

Competitor comparisons must distinguish:

- directly verified fact;
- competitor-stated claim;
- customer-reported experience;
- internal evidence;
- inference.

Do not call a competitor "better," "worse," "cheaper," "faster," or "less safe" without evidence appropriate to the claim.

## Testimonials & Social Proof

Testimonials, reviews, quotes, logos, case-study claims, and creator/influencer endorsements must be authentic and attributable where required.

Do not fabricate testimonials or materially alter their meaning.

Identify disclosure/permission requirements where applicable.

## Intellectual Property

Verify rights or permission when using:

- third-party images/video/audio;
- logos and trademarks;
- customer materials;
- quotes or excerpts;
- competitor assets;
- proprietary research or datasets.

Do not assume that publicly accessible content is automatically free to reuse.

## Platform & Policy Risk

Platform-specific rules can change. When compliance is material, verify current official platform policy/documentation rather than relying on memory or a legacy playbook.

Distinguish:

- platform requirement;
- platform recommendation;
- strategist recommendation;
- experiment hypothesis.

## Escalation

Escalate to qualified human/legal review when:

- the claim has material legal/financial/health/safety impact;
- the relevant law/policy is uncertain;
- a regulated activity is involved;
- the proposed language could create contractual or consumer liability;
- the model lacks sufficient jurisdiction/context to assess the issue;
- the cost of being wrong is materially higher than the cost of review.

Do not block ordinary low-risk creative work merely because legal review exists; scale review to the risk.

## Strategic Boundary

Compliance analysis informs strategy but does not silently redefine the business objective or positioning. When a legal/compliance constraint materially changes the viable strategic options, return the constraint and implications to Strategic Intelligence for synthesis.

## Quality Gate

Before finalizing a material recommendation:

- [ ] Claims are evidence-backed and not guarantees without basis.
- [ ] Material assumptions/qualifiers are visible.
- [ ] Regulated or high-risk claims are identified.
- [ ] Privacy/data implications are considered where relevant.
- [ ] Comparative claims are verified where relevant.
- [ ] Testimonials/endorsements are authentic and attributable.
- [ ] IP/usage rights are considered where relevant.
- [ ] Current platform/policy requirements are verified when material.
- [ ] Jurisdiction-specific uncertainty is disclosed.
- [ ] Human/legal escalation is recommended when risk warrants it.
