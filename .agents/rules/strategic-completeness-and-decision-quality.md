# Strategic Completeness & Decision Quality

## Purpose

Apply this rule to `strategic-intelligence` and any executive-level strategic synthesis. It prevents a marketing diagnosis from becoming a narrow communications plan when the underlying business problem may sit elsewhere.

## Strategic Scope

For broad or decision-oriented work, scan the relevant business system before deciding that marketing is the primary lever:

1. **Business model** — customer, problem, value creation, revenue model, cost structure, key activities/resources, partners, and channels.
2. **Market** — demand, category, market structure, trends, seasonality, geography, regulation, substitutes, and external forces.
3. **Customer** — segments, jobs, needs, triggers, decision criteria, objections, willingness-to-buy signals, retention drivers, and customer economics.
4. **Competition** — alternatives, competitors, differentiation, switching costs, defensibility, positioning, distribution, and evidence of competitor effectiveness.
5. **Offer & monetization** — product/service, value proposition, pricing, packaging, margins, commercial terms, and purchase friction.
6. **Brand & communications** — awareness, associations, positioning, message, proof, reputation, and channel/media role.
7. **Demand generation & funnel** — discovery, acquisition, qualification, conversion, onboarding/activation, retention, expansion, referral, and sales handoff.
8. **Operations & capability** — team, skills, capacity, systems, inventory/delivery constraints, technology, partners, and execution feasibility.
9. **Economics** — revenue drivers, gross margin/contribution logic where available, CAC, LTV, payback, conversion economics, budget constraints, and sensitivity to assumptions.
10. **Risk & constraints** — legal/regulatory, reputational, operational, financial, channel/platform, dependency, and execution risks when material.
11. **Strategic alternatives** — plausible options, including doing less, changing the offer, changing segment, changing channel, improving operations, or not pursuing the opportunity.

Not every dimension must be deeply researched. Use a **relevance test** and document dimensions that are not material to the decision.

## Diagnosis Before Prescription

Before recommending an initiative, determine:

- What outcome matters?
- What decision must be made?
- What is the observed situation?
- What constraints shape the decision?
- What evidence supports each important conclusion?
- What are the plausible competing explanations?
- What would have to be true for each option to work?
- What is the highest-leverage constraint?
- What evidence would disconfirm the current diagnosis?

Do not allow a user's requested tactic to become the problem definition.

## Problem Decomposition

Use a layered diagnosis when appropriate:

**Symptom → immediate cause → underlying cause → structural constraint → strategic implication.**

Example:

`Low sales → low conversion → low qualified demand OR offer mismatch OR sales-process friction → underlying constraint → strategic choice`

Do not stop at the first measurable symptom.

## Alternative Hypotheses

For every major diagnosis, consider at least one credible alternative explanation where ambiguity is material.

For each important hypothesis record:

- Evidence supporting it
- Evidence against it
- Confidence
- What would change the conclusion
- Lowest-cost validation test

Avoid confirmation bias from the user's framing, prior strategy documents, or favorite marketing framework.

## Strategic Choice Quality

A recommendation should make the trade-off explicit when material:

| Dimension | Ask |
|---|---|
| Impact | What business outcome could move? |
| Confidence | How strong is the evidence? |
| Feasibility | Can the organization execute it? |
| Economics | Does the expected value justify the resources/risk? |
| Time to value | How soon can the decision produce useful learning or outcome? |
| Reversibility | Can the organization undo the decision cheaply? |
| Dependency | What must be true first? |
| Strategic fit | Does it strengthen or weaken the broader business position? |
| Option value | Does it create useful future choices or lock the business in? |

Do not use a fake precision score when evidence cannot support one.

## Scenario & Sensitivity Thinking

When a recommendation depends heavily on uncertain variables, use scenario or sensitivity analysis rather than one-point certainty.

Possible variables include:
- demand volume
- conversion rate
- average order/deal value
- gross margin
- CAC
- retention/churn
- sales capacity
- channel availability
- price acceptance
- delivery capacity

Use ranges or qualitative scenarios when exact numbers are unavailable. Label them as assumptions, not forecasts.

## Research Design

Research should answer decisions, not merely collect information.

For each major research question define:

- Decision supported
- Evidence required
- Source priority
- Freshness requirement
- Triangulation need
- Minimum sufficient evidence
- Stopping condition

Prefer the cheapest reliable evidence that can reduce the decision uncertainty. Do not run broad research when a focused answer is sufficient.

## Channel / Media Decision Quality

When evaluating channels, consider both **market fit** and **execution fit**.

Market fit:
- audience presence
- intent/context
- demand signals
- competitive environment
- reach
- discovery mechanics
- customer journey role

Execution fit:
- economics
- conversion path
- attribution
- creative/content requirements
- operational complexity
- internal capability
- speed to learn
- platform dependency/risk

A channel with large reach but poor conversion path or weak organizational capability may be lower priority than a smaller channel with stronger economics and fit.

## Economics Guardrail

Never state that a strategy is commercially attractive without enough inputs to support that conclusion.

When economics are incomplete:

- identify missing inputs;
- provide formulas or sensitivity logic where useful;
- use directional conclusions only;
- explicitly label provisional assumptions.

Do not invent market size, TAM, CAC, LTV, margins, ROAS, conversion rates, revenue, or ROI.

## Strategic Alternatives & Stop Decisions

The recommendation set must include the possibility of:

- **Proceed**
- **Test**
- **Investigate**
- **Delay**
- **Stop / do not pursue**
- **Change the underlying strategy**

The objective is not to maximize the amount of activity. It is to choose the highest-value path under current evidence and constraints.

## Executive Output Quality Gate

Before finalizing a broad strategic diagnosis, verify:

- [ ] Business objective and decision are explicit.
- [ ] Marketing is not assumed to be the primary problem.
- [ ] Business model and commercial logic were considered where relevant.
- [ ] Customer, market, competitor, offer, funnel, and capability context were considered where relevant.
- [ ] Important economics or missing inputs are visible.
- [ ] Constraints and risks are explicit.
- [ ] Major alternative hypotheses were considered.
- [ ] Recommendation has a clear evidence basis and confidence.
- [ ] Strategic trade-offs are explicit.
- [ ] Research is decision-oriented and not open-ended.
- [ ] At least one disconfirming condition is known for major decisions.
- [ ] Appropriate decision type is stated: proceed/test/investigate/delay/stop/change.
- [ ] Specialist routing is downstream of the diagnosis.

## Boundary

This rule does not require a full consulting-style analysis for every small request. Scale the depth to the decision's stakes, uncertainty, reversibility, and cross-functional impact.
