# Viral Launch Playbook

A launch-distribution playbook for X. Use it as an execution and experimentation layer, not as a substitute for business strategy, customer research, positioning, or channel selection.

## Strategic Position in the System

For broad, ambiguous, cross-functional, or decision-oriented requests, consume `/strategic-intelligence` first.

Strategic Intelligence is the source of truth for:
- business objective and decision
- audience and journey context
- evidence and confidence
- diagnosed bottleneck/opportunity
- positioning and offer implications
- channel/media role
- priorities, constraints, and measurement

Do not assume X is the right channel, that a launch is the right intervention, or that virality is the right objective. If the strategic diagnosis points to another channel or upstream intervention, surface that instead of forcing this playbook.

For a narrow X-launch execution request where strategy is already established, proceed with the supplied context.

## Core Principles

1. **Outcome before virality.** Define the business or communication outcome before optimizing for reach or engagement.
2. **Evidence before claims.** Treat platform behavior, audience response, competitor activity, and performance patterns as evidence only when actually observed or sourced.
3. **Distinctive positioning over manufactured controversy.** Strong hooks can be emotionally resonant without baiting, attacking, or manufacturing conflict.
4. **Experimentation over certainty.** Platform distribution is dynamic. Test hypotheses instead of claiming a fixed algorithmic formula.
5. **Sustainable execution.** Do not require unhealthy or impossible monitoring schedules. Define coverage, ownership, and response windows that fit the team.

Minimum launch context:

- Launch timing, if known
- Strategic reason for using X, if already decided
- Available assets/resources
- Constraints and compliance requirements

Do not require X API access or an influencer network unless the requested tactic actually depends on them.

## Step 1: Define the Outcome and Hook Hypothesis

Start with the audience problem, desired behavior/perception, and relevant value—not merely product features.

Use a feature → capability → outcome chain:

`Feature → enables capability → creates customer-relevant outcome`

Then develop one or more hook hypotheses that express the most relevant outcome or tension.

Do not claim that an emotional outcome is automatically the positioning. Positioning should come from the validated strategic context.

### Hook quality checks

Evaluate:
- Relevance to the intended audience
- Clarity
- Distinctiveness
- Credibility
- Evidence/proof
- Emotional or intellectual interest
- Fit with the actual offer

Do not require arbitrary 10/10 scores. Explain trade-offs and improve weak dimensions.

## Step 2: Engineer the Launch Copy

Review the launch copy for:
- clear value
- specific audience relevance
- credible differentiation
- proof or reason-to-believe
- strong opening
- readable structure
- appropriate CTA
- factual accuracy

Claims such as "world's first," "best," "fastest," "guaranteed," or quantified results require evidence. Otherwise rewrite them as appropriately qualified claims.

Avoid fabricated customer results, competitor attacks, false scarcity, or manufactured controversy.

## Step 3: Research Audience and Conversation Patterns

When research is needed, investigate the actual conversation around the category and audience.

Possible evidence sources include:
- X posts and replies
- customer interviews/VOC
- existing campaign data
- relevant community discussions
- competitor/channel observations
- other current sources appropriate to the decision

Separate:
- **Observed:** what the data actually shows
- **Inference:** what the pattern may mean
- **Hypothesis:** what should be tested

Competitor activity or high engagement does **not** prove that a tactic caused success or that it will work for this product.

Do not use engagement thresholds such as a fixed number of likes or quote tweets as universal definitions of a viral post. Define useful thresholds from the account/category baseline when data exists.

## Step 4: Match the Message to Proven Formats

Identify content formats and framings that are relevant to the audience and objective.

Research should compare current and historical examples where possible, but do not treat the highest-viewed post/video as automatically causal or reproducible.

Extract structural patterns such as:
- problem framing
- narrative structure
- demonstration
- contrast
- proof
- objection handling
- curiosity
- audience participation

Adapt patterns rather than copying claims or assuming the same performance ceiling.

## Step 5: Design Distribution Experiments

Create a distribution plan appropriate to the available resources.

Possible tactics:
- founder/account replies
- relevant conversations
- community participation
- partner or creator amplification
- customer advocacy
- follow-up content
- paid amplification when strategically justified

If influencers/partners are used:
- select them for audience relevance and credibility, not follower count alone
- disclose paid/sponsored relationships as required
- define the hypothesis and measurement before activation

Do not deliberately provoke people, manufacture conflict, or target "haters" merely to create engagement.

## Step 6: Launch and Learn

Use a launch window appropriate to the campaign, team, and platform context. There is no universal 48-hour requirement.

During launch:
1. Monitor agreed leading indicators.
2. Respond to meaningful questions and objections.
3. Record notable audience reactions and objections.
4. Avoid changing too many variables at once.
5. Escalate factual, safety, legal, or reputation risks.
6. Preserve evidence so the next decision is based on what actually happened.

Do not claim that the first few hours determine whether a post will succeed, or that any single algorithmic mechanism guarantees distribution. Treat early performance as a signal, not a verdict.

## Measurement

Connect launch measurement to the strategic objective.

Possible hierarchy:
1. Business outcome
2. Campaign outcome
3. Audience behavior/perception
4. Distribution indicators
5. Engagement diagnostics

Examples of diagnostic metrics may include reach, qualified engagement, profile actions, clicks, signups, conversions, or assisted outcomes, but the relevant metrics depend on the objective.

Do not invent targets, attribution, conversion rates, or ROI. Establish baselines or provisional thresholds where needed.

## Output Format

Adapt the deliverables to the actual task. Possible outputs:

- `01-positioning-hook.md` — hook hypotheses and rationale
- `02-launch-copy.md` — launch copy with evidence/claim review
- `03-reply-bank.md` — response patterns for likely audience reactions
- `04-content-calendar.md` — follow-up content sequence
- `05-launch-plan.md` — launch sequence, owners, dependencies, experiments, and measurement

Include a **Gaps & Assumptions** section whenever important inputs remain unverified.

## Evidence Discipline

Use explicit evidence classes:
- **FACT** — directly supported by reliable evidence/context
- **OBSERVATION** — observed pattern in supplied/researched data
- **INFERENCE** — reasoned interpretation
- **HYPOTHESIS** — plausible but unvalidated explanation
- **BENCHMARK** — external reference with source/context
- **RECOMMENDATION** — proposed action based on the above

Never invent performance metrics, customer results, market facts, competitor claims, or algorithmic guarantees.

## Specialist Boundary

This skill owns launch-on-X execution tactics and experimentation. It does not override Strategic Intelligence on:
- whether a launch is the right intervention
- whether X is the right channel
- strategic audience selection
- business priorities
- positioning direction
- major resource trade-offs

If new evidence materially changes the diagnosis, return the issue to Strategic Intelligence.

## Anti-Patterns

Do not:
- treat virality as the objective by default
- assume X is strategically appropriate
- present platform folklore as algorithmic fact
- manufacture controversy or bait harassment
- use fixed engagement thresholds as universal rules
- promise viral outcomes
- require nonstop monitoring or unhealthy work schedules
- fabricate customer/competitor/performance claims
- copy another account's results as if they were reproducible
- restart strategic diagnosis unnecessarily when a valid handoff exists
